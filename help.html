How it works

How do I read an alert?

Every alert has the same four parts. Here's a real one from November 10:

  1. Who and what. The player, his team, his status, and how long he's out if the report says. Next to that is his average fantasy points per game (FPTS/g).
  2. The news. One plain-English line.
  3. Pickups. Up to three players we project to gain, each with a tag.
  4. Source. Where the report came from.

Statuses you'll see: OUT, Doubtful, Questionable, Left game, Won't return.

What do the tags mean?

The + number next to a pickup is how many more fantasy points per game we project for him while the injured player is out. We base it on how teammates played when he sat before and who usually takes his minutes. The tag tells you which league sizes he's worth a look in.

🔥All leagues+8 or more
🎯12+ team+5 to +8
👀Deep league+3 to +5

We leave out pickups under +3. We also leave out anyone already averaging 32+ fantasy points a game, because he's rostered everywhere.

Does it fit my league's scoring?

Every number uses this points scoring:

  • Point1
  • Rebound1.2
  • Assist1.5
  • Steal3
  • Block3
  • Three made0.5
  • Turnover-1

If your league scores differently or uses categories, treat the numbers as a rough guide.

Where does the news come from?

Reporters on X, and how often we check them:

  • Shams Charania and Underdog NBAAbout every 90 seconds
  • Other main insidersEvery 10 minutes
  • Beat writersAbout every 50 minutes

We also read the official NBA injury report, ESPN, RotoWire, CBS Sports and Bluesky.

What gets posted?

  • A player is ruled out, or listed doubtful or questionable.
  • A player who's already out gets a new timeline, like "out 2-3 weeks".
  • A player leaves a game. If he comes back within about four hours, we reply under that post.
  • Each night, a recap thread: top performers, breakouts and minutes to watch.

Can I trust it?

An AI model reads each report, decides the status and writes the one-line summary. Code then checks that line against the original report and rejects it if it names a team, day, body part or timeline the report doesn't.

Mistakes still happen, in the summaries and in the pickups. Every alert names its source, so check it before a move you can't undo.
